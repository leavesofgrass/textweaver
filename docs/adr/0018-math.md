# ADR-0018: Math

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-09-25
- Status update (Saturday, September 26, 2026): Integrated as described under Integration, with three differences. In `tw convert`, what counts as math is decided by the Markdown engine's own dollar rules (pulldown-cmark or comrak), not by `find_math`, so with the default engine some prices, such as "$5-$10", can become MathML. Fenced code blocks marked `am` count as ASCIIMath as well as `asciimath`. The GUI spike and `tw speak` do not pass `math_verbosity` or `asciimath_delimiter` on. The app has no math exploration mode yet: `Navigator` is ready in the library, but no reader key uses it.
- Status update (Saturday, September 26, 2026): the Markdown loader marks math (`Math` markers), and every writer typesets it. `tw speak` reads the settings now, so it follows `math_verbosity` and `asciimath_delimiter`; the GUI spike still does not. The reader still has no math exploration mode.

- Status update (Sunday, September 27, 2026): MathCAT can speak math instead, behind the `mathcat` feature and the `[reading] math_engine` setting, with this engine as the default and the fallback (ADR-0029). `speak_text_with` asks another engine first for each region. EPUB 3 MathML is read as math, through LaTeX under a `Math` marker.

## Context

Students meet math in LaTeX (`$…$` in Markdown, Pandoc, and papers) and in ASCIIMath (course platforms). Star handled it twice, separately. Its speech path (`star/ttstext/mathspeech.py`) is a chain of regular expressions that runs over all text, so `snake_case` became "snake sub case", `[^1]` became "to the 1", `\$3.99` lost its "three", and `$5 and $10` lost its dollar signs (Star bugs Q6 to Q8). Its display path (`star/mathrender.py`) turned LaTeX into Unicode approximations. Neither kept a mapping from spoken words to the source, and neither understood structure, so a fraction inside a root inside a fraction could not be read unambiguously or explored.

The primary user reads by ear. Spoken math has to be natural, precise enough to write the expression back down, and navigable part by part.

## Decision

A standalone crate, `textweaver-math`, depending only on core, `thiserror`, and `serde`, with hand-written parsers.

**One tree for both notations.** `parse_latex` and `parse_asciimath` produce a `Math` whose `Node`s each carry the char range of their source. Node kinds: row, number, identifier, function, operator (with a class: binary, relation, large, open, close, fence, punctuation, prefix, postfix), text, space, fraction (with or without bar), root, scripts (side or limits), fenced, accent, table (matrix, cases, aligned, array), style, enclosure, and error. Both parsers are total: every input yields a tree, recovery is recorded as `Diagnostic`s, `Math::strict` turns them into a `MathError`, nesting deeper than 48 levels becomes error nodes, and property tests (20,000 cases run locally) show no panics.

- LaTeX: the common subset: groups, scripts and primes, `\frac` family, `\binom`, `\sqrt[n]`, 264 symbol commands including Greek letters (plus aliases such as `\le`), big operators with `\limits`/`\nolimits`, named functions and `\operatorname`, `\left…\middle…\right`, `\big` sizes, environments (`matrix`, `pmatrix`, `bmatrix`, `Bmatrix`, `vmatrix`, `Vmatrix`, `smallmatrix`, `array`, `cases`, `rcases`, `aligned`, `align`, `gather`, `split`, `equation`), `\\` line breaks, `\text`, fonts, accents, `\overset`/`\underset`, `\not`, `\pmod`, `\cancel`, `\boxed`, spacing, `\over`/`\choose`. Brackets typed as characters (`(a+b)`, `|x|`, `\lfloor x \rfloor`) are paired into fenced nodes after each row, and scripts on a closing bracket move to the whole group.
- ASCIIMath: the grammar and full symbol table of the reference `ASCIIMathML.js`, including the LaTeX names it copies in (378 inputs), longest-match tokens, bracket removal around arguments, `I/I` fractions, matrices from `[[a,b],[c,d]]`, and cases from `{(…),(…):}`. A unit test parses every table entry.

**MathML.** `to_mathml` writes presentation MathML with `alttext` (the source by default, or spoken English) and a `<semantics>` annotation holding the source. Display math puts sum and product limits below and above; integrals keep side limits.

**Speech.** `speak` produces ClearSpeak-style English at three verbosity levels, built with `SpokenBuilder` (ADR-0005): words equal to their source are literal spans, words standing for source are expanded spans, structural words are inserted. Low is terse ("a over b", "x to the n"); normal is the default and follows ClearSpeak ("the fraction with numerator x plus 1 and denominator 2", "x to the n-th power", "the absolute value of x"); high adds articles, end markers, "cap" before capital letters, font names, and column numbers. End markers are otherwise spoken only when a part is not simple and something follows it. Where English order differs from source order ("x hat" for `\hat{x}`), the out-of-order word becomes an inserted span so the map stays valid. The wording table is in the `speech` module's documentation.

**Navigation.** `Navigator` moves through the tree: next and previous part, first and last, into a part or into a named role (numerator, denominator, index, radicand, base, subscript, superscript, lower and upper limit, contents, table cell), and out. Each move returns a `NavStep` with the role, the part spoken on its own with its map, and its source span, or `None` at a boundary.

**Detection.** `find_math` finds `$…$`, `$$…$$`, `\(…\)`, `\[…\]`, and, when enabled, ASCIIMath between a configurable delimiter (a backtick by convention; off by default because a backtick in Markdown is code). Inline dollars follow Pandoc's `tex_math_dollars` rule: a non-space after the opening `$`, a non-space before the closing one, no digit right after it, and only the first following `$` is a candidate. `\$` never delimits; no pair crosses a blank line. `speak_text` is the normalization transform: it replaces each region with its spoken form, elides the delimiters, and leaves all other text literal, so prose is never treated as math.

### Integration

Done (2026-09-25) as planned below, with these specifics: the speech `Math` transform runs first in the pipeline, before Markdown residue and the lexicons as well as abbreviations and numbers, so every later step sees words rather than LaTeX; it still reads a few operator symbols in prose (`×`, `≤`, `→`) as Star did. Settings are `[normalization] math_verbosity` (`low`, `normal`, `high`) and `asciimath_delimiter` (a single character, unset by default). The renderer calls `parse_latex` or `parse_asciimath` and `to_mathml` (what `latex_to_mathml` does), but shows a formula the parser had to repair as its source in `<code class="math-error">`, as it did with `pulldown-latex`. ASCIIMath reaches the renderer from code fences marked `asciimath` and, opt-in, from inline code spans. `pulldown-latex` is gone from the workspace.

- **Speech pipeline.** Replace the regex rules in `textweaver-speech/src/normalize/math.rs` with a `Transform` whose `apply` is `textweaver_math::speak_text(input, &TextOptions { detect, speech: SpeechOptions::new(verbosity) })`; the returned `(String, OffsetMap)` already maps output bytes to input chars. Run it **before** `Numbers` (and abbreviations): `Numbers` turns `$2` into currency words, which destroys `$2x$`, and math speech already leaves digits literal for the engine or for `Numbers` to read. Take `DetectOptions::asciimath` and the math verbosity from settings (`[normalization] math_verbosity`, `asciimath_delimiter`). Keep `math` in the pipeline for engines that normalize natively; no engine reads LaTeX.
- **Renderer.** Enable pulldown-cmark's `ENABLE_MATH` and replace `pulldown-latex` with `textweaver_math::latex_to_mathml(src, display)` for `Event::InlineMath` and `Event::DisplayMath` (comrak: the math nodes' literal text and `display_math` flag). For ASCIIMath spans in Obsidian or course material, `asciimath_to_mathml`. Use `to_mathml` with `MathMlOptions { alttext: AltText::Speech(Verbosity::Normal), .. }` if screen-reader users without MathML support should hear English rather than LaTeX. The output is a single `<math>` element; no stylesheet or script is needed.
- **App (later).** A math-exploration mode can hold a `Math` (from `find_math` at the cursor plus `parse`), drive a `Navigator` from the arrow keys, speak `NavStep::announcement`, and highlight `NavStep::span` shifted by the region's content start.

### Star parity

Star's math vectors (`tests/test_ttstext.py`) pass at normal verbosity, with two deliberate wording changes: `\alpha + \beta` is "alpha plus beta" (Star left `+` to the engine, which drops it at low punctuation levels) and `\bar{x}` is "x bar" (Star: "x-bar"). Its undelimited vectors (`x^2 and y^{3}`) are math only when delimited, as in documents (`$x^2$ and $y^{3}$` → "x squared and y cubed"). Bugs fixed: Q6 (subscripts in prose), Q7 (trailing `x^2`), Q8 (global brace stripping, space collapsing, and `$5 and $10`), and the Pandoc `\$3.99` case; each has a test.

## Consequences

- Highlighting and pause/resume are exact inside math, like everywhere else.
- Math speech is independent of the engine: digits and letters reach the engine literally; everything structural is words.
- The parsers are ours to extend. Unsupported LaTeX is spoken by its command name and reported, not dropped silently.
- The wording is ClearSpeak-like but not a full ClearSpeak implementation (no preference settings for trigonometry, powers, or sets beyond the three levels). A MathCAT integration could replace `speech` later behind the same `Spoken` output if its wording is preferred.
