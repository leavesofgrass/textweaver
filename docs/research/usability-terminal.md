# Usability pass: the terminal reader and `tw`

A pass over `textweaver` (the terminal reader) and `tw` (the command line) from a screen reader user's point of view, on Saturday, September 26, 2026, between Wave 3 and Wave 4 (Agent UX-1, branch `ux/terminal-pass`). The GUI has its own pass.

How it was done: the code that produces every announcement, prompt, list, and error was read against the docs (`quickstart.md`, `reading.md`, `editing.md`, `notes.md`, `screen-readers.md`, `keyboard.md`, `troubleshooting.md`); the reader was driven headlessly on ratatui's test backend with the recording speech backend, so nothing was played aloud; and `tw` was run with a throwaway `--home` and the silent backend. Nothing was tried by ear with NVDA or JAWS: that is the owner's check, step 3 of the pass.

Each finding below has its evidence, its size, and whether it was a quick win. The quick wins are fixed on the branch, with tests. The rest is ranked by its impact on a blind user.

## What was fixed

1. **A yes-or-no question asked while textweaver reads aloud was never spoken.** `Ctrl+Q`, Delete on a note, "Reload it?", "Replace it?", the Piper download and every other question went through the polite announcer, and polite announcements are not spoken while reading (`App::say_at`). In self-voicing mode the reading went on, the question sat on the status line unheard, and the next key press was swallowed as the answer. Fix: a new `App::ask` announces questions assertively, so they are spoken over the reading and the reading resumes; every question site uses it. Test: `usability::a_question_asked_while_reading_is_spoken`.
2. **The first run said nothing beyond "Opened".** A new user with no screen reader had no way to learn Space, Escape, `h`, F1, and `Ctrl+Q` except the docs. Fix: on the first run under a state folder (no settings file, no keymap file, no saved document state) the reader queues one welcome sentence after "Opened", with the keys named from the keymap in effect (`setup::first_run_message`). It is not heard again once a document's state or a settings file exists. Test: `setup::tests::the_welcome_is_for_the_first_run_only`.
3. **Startup warnings cut each other off.** Each message from loading settings and the keymap was announced assertively in turn, and an assertive announcement interrupts the one before, so only the last was heard and "Opened" was lost. Fix: they are queued after "Opened" and share the status line with it (`launch`).
4. **"Could not open" read an operating system error, with the path twice.** `textweaver nothere.md` said "Could not open nothere.md: cannot read nothere.md: The system cannot find the file specified. (os error 2)"; a folder said "Access is denied. (os error 5)". `tw text` printed the same reason three times as a chain of causes. Fix: `open_failure_reason` in the app crate turns a missing file, a folder, and a permission failure into one sentence, strips the error code from any other, and is used by the reader (opening at once and in the background), `tw text`, `tw info`, `tw search`, and `tw open`, which now refuses a folder before the reader starts. Tests: `usability::open_failures_are_plain_sentences`, `text::tests::open_failures_are_one_plain_sentence`, `open::tests::a_folder_is_not_a_document`.
5. **F1 did not say how to open a document, nor the quick navigation keys.** The help listed "Read the next and previous heading: > and <" but not `h`, `1` to `6`, `l`, `t`, `k`, and the rest, nor `Ctrl+O`, `Alt+L`, `Alt+Shift+A`, F9, the settings screen, `Alt+V`, or `Shift+F8`. It also read every chord of every action ("Ctrl+P or p or ] or Ctrl+Down or PageDown and Shift+P or [ or Ctrl+Up or PageUp"), which cannot be followed by ear, and "Tab or Tab" for Speech Cursor, bound in two layers. Fix: new lines for all of these; each line names at most two keys, the single key and one chord that works with single-key shortcuts off (`short_chords_text`); with single-key shortcuts off the chord comes first; chords are named once. Tests: `usability::the_help_names_the_keys_a_new_user_needs`, `help::tests::one_key_per_action_spoken_and_written`.
6. **"Press Control O to open one" was a fixed string.** With `open` rebound it lied. Fix: `spoken_key` takes the key from the keymap, in its spoken form ("Control O"), for the no-document messages of the reader and of every reading key. Test: `usability::the_no_document_message_names_the_open_key`.
7. **`textweaver --help` began "textweaver terminal UI: self-voicing ratatui frontend".** Jargon for the first thing a new user reads. Fix: it now says what it reads and the first keys to press.
8. **`tw text big.pdf | head` panicked** with "failed printing to stdout: The pipe has been ended" once `head` closed the pipe. Fix: `print_all` ends quietly on a closed pipe; `tw text` and `tw search` use it.
9. **`tw define` reported a miss twice**: "No definition found for x." then "Error: no definition found". Fix: the first message only, exit status 1.
10. **`tw marks` had no `--home`**, unlike every other command that reads the state folder, and `troubleshooting.md` said it did. Fix: added.
11. **Three doc passages were stale.** `reading.md` said `q` closes a list and `j` and `k` move (a letter jumps to the next item now; Escape and Backspace close). `notes.md` said Delete in the notes and highlights lists deletes at once (it asks first). `editing.md` gave the unsaved-changes prompt without the `s`, `d`, `c` letters it announces. Fixed.

## What is left, ranked by impact on a blind user

Sizes: small is under half a day, medium a day or two, large more.

1. **Keys are read in their written form in self-voicing mode** (medium). The help, the keyboard list, the palette, and messages such as "Edit mode on. Save: Ctrl+S." give chords as `Alt+.`, `Shift+W`, `\`. A screen reader reading the status line says "alt plus dot", which is fine; textweaver's own voice depends on the punctuation level, and at "some" or "none" `Alt+.` becomes "alt" and `\` becomes nothing. `KeyChord::spoken` already exists ("Alt period"). Route: when the message goes to the voice, use the spoken form; when it goes to the status line only, the written one. The status-line copy could keep the written form. Evidence: `help.rs`, `chord.rs`.
2. **The title line says "Stopped" before anything was read** (small). A screen reader reading the title line hears "Stopped" at startup. "Ready" until the first play, then "Stopped", would mislead nobody. Evidence: `ui.rs draw_title`.
3. **No way to hear the title line or the status line again on demand** (small). `Shift+W` says the position, but not the mode, rate, and engine the title line shows, and there is no key to repeat the last announcement. Screen reader users have "read current line"; self-voicing users have nothing. A "say status" action (repeat the last message, then the title line's parts) would be one action and one browse key.
4. **The command palette's prompt is one word, "Command"** (small). A first-time user does not learn that Tab completes, Up and Down list matches, and part of a name is enough. Say it once when the palette opens: "Command. Type part of a name; Tab completes, Up and Down list matches." The label drawn on the bottom line can stay short.
5. **Escape in edit mode does nothing a user can hear** (small). Escape is Stop everywhere; in edit mode with nothing playing it is silent, and users used to editors expect it to leave. Either say "Still editing. Ctrl+E finishes." on Escape in edit mode, or leave edit mode when there are no unsaved changes.
6. **`tw` with no arguments prints the whole command list as an error** (small). Twenty-one commands scroll past a screen reader with exit status 2. A two-line hint ("tw open FILE reads a document aloud; tw --help lists every command") would be kinder; clap's `arg_required_else_help` is the current behaviour.
7. **Unbound keys are silent** (small, but a decision). Pressing a letter that does nothing gives no feedback, as in NVDA's browse mode. A short tone (the `Error` earcon exists) at high verbosity would tell a user the key reached textweaver and meant nothing. NVDA is silent here too, so this is a choice for the owner.
8. **A long list's introduction is not repeatable** (small). "Notes, 12 notes. Enter goes to a note, Delete deletes it, F2 edits it." is heard once; there is no key to hear the list's title and count again while it is open. Home already says the first item; a key for "where am I in this list" would help.
9. **The saved-changes list and the recovery list are the only lists with letter accelerators** (medium). Other lists jump by first letter, which is right; but the export and citation lists could take `y` and `n` for "Open it?" questions consistently. The wording is consistent already.
10. **`tw search` and `tw info` still panic on a closed pipe for JSON output** (small). `print_all` covers the text output; the `--json` branches use `println!`.
11. **Startup is silent while the speech engine starts** (medium). The engine starts on a helper thread and the reader is "silent until the engine is ready"; "Opened" is announced before the engine is up, so with a slow engine the first announcements are lost to the null service. The status line has them, so a screen reader user is fine; a self-voicing user hears nothing until the engine reports. Queue the announcements made before the engine is ready and say them once it is.
12. **Docs say `q` quits in the classic preset but the quick start says only `Ctrl+Q`** (small). Both are true; the quick start could say "with the classic keys, `q` too". The owner's decision (q asks first; y quits; n or a cancel) is what the code does for `Ctrl+Q` and for `q` in the classic preset.

## Suggested items for Wave 4

- Spoken key names when textweaver's own voice reads a key (item 1). This touches the app's message building only, so it fits W4d (interface translations), which is already rewording messages through the catalog.
- A "say status" action and a "repeat last message" action (item 3), with browse keys, in both frontends.
- "Ready" on the title line before the first play, and Escape in edit mode saying how to finish (items 2 and 5).
- Queue announcements made before the speech engine is ready (item 11) in W4b (speed), which owns startup.
- The owner's check, step 3: the first-run question with NVDA and JAWS, whether the welcome comes through once, and whether a question asked during reading is now heard over the voice in Windows Terminal and the classic console.
- A friendlier `tw` with no arguments (item 6).
