# Listening as a Study Skill

Students who read with a screen reader or a braille display study as much
by ear and by touch as by sight.[^1] This paper looks at how listening
supports study, and at what an accessible paper needs to carry that
support to its reader.

## Method

Twelve students kept a reading log for four weeks. Each entry named the
document, the tool used to read it, and whether the structure (headings,
lists, tables, and notes) came through.[^2]

### Materials

| Tool | Documents read | Structure kept |
|---|---|---|
| Screen reader | 48 | 41 |
| Braille display | 22 | 20 |

### Procedure

1. Open the document in the usual reader.
2. Move by heading to the method section.
3. Read the first footnote and return.

## Results

Headings and footnotes were the structure students used most. As one
student wrote, "a footnote I can jump to is a footnote I actually read."[^1]

## Discussion

Accessible structure is part of the argument of a paper, not decoration
added afterwards.
