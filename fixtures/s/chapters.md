# Chapter one: Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis lets green plants turn sunlight into chemical energy.
The process takes place in the chloroplasts of leaf cells.
Chlorophyll in the chloroplasts absorbs the sunlight that photosynthesis needs.

Plants use that energy to make sugar from water and carbon dioxide.
Oxygen is released into the air as a by-product of photosynthesis.
The gardener next door painted her fence blue last summer.
Sugar made by photosynthesis feeds the plant and the animals that eat plants.

# Chapter two: The water cycle

Water moves between the oceans, the air, and the land in the water cycle.
The sun warms the oceans, and water evaporates into the air as vapor.
Water vapor cools high in the air and condenses into clouds.
Our train was late on Tuesday because of a signal problem.

Clouds release the water as rain or snow when the droplets grow heavy.
Rain that falls on the land runs into rivers that carry the water back to the oceans.
Some of the water soaks into the ground and feeds the plants on its way.
